# Web pages: search and AI search

Read only when the user says the text is for a site and should rank or be cited. Priority 1 of the skill still wins: if a rule here makes the page worse for a person, drop it.

## How search engines treat AI text
Google says it doesn't rank text down for being AI-written; it demotes scaled content abuse: many pages made to rank, with no value, however they were made. Yandex has no public statement on AI text, but its "low-value or low-demand page" status works the same way in practice. Both reward pages that help the person who searched.

## Articles and guides
- **Add what only this author has.** Own numbers, test results, photos, prices, cases, "we tried X and Y happened". Ask the user for them; never invent them.
- **Answer the query in the first lines,** then detail. No warm-up about why the topic matters, no «В этой статье мы рассмотрим…».
- **Show who wrote it and why they know:** name, role, experience, if the user provides them.
- **Visible update date,** and no figures that go stale (prices, "in 2026") without one.
- **No keyword stuffing.** Use the query phrase where it reads naturally.

## Product and category pages
A product page is scanned, not read: "interesting" here means the buyer finds the answer fast.
- Lead with what this item does differently from its neighbours in the catalogue, then specs, then who it suits, what's in the box, delivery, warranty.
- Don't rewrite the manufacturer's text with synonyms. Add what the shop knows: who it suits, what buyers ask, how it compares with the next model. If there's nothing to add, write a short spec-based description, not filler.
- Trust comes from the shop, not an author: concrete terms ("delivery in Moscow tomorrow, 2-year warranty, 14-day returns"), not "fast delivery and reliable warranty". Use the user's real terms.
- A category text is short, helps choose within the category (how items differ, what to look at), and doesn't push the listing down.
- **Series of similar pages** (cities, models, categories) must differ in facts, not in swapped names. For Yandex, near-identical cards risk the "low-value or low-demand" status in Webmaster.

## Title, description, H1, alt
Same rules as the body: specific, no stock phrases («лучшие цены», «широкий ассортимент», "high quality"). The title names the thing and one real differentiator. Facts in the text, title, structured data and product feed (price, availability, model, size) must match. If AI-written product titles or descriptions go into Google Merchant Center, tell the user they must be submitted in the AI-labelled attributes.

## Being cited by AI search
Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special optimization and advises against splitting content into bite-sized chunks for LLMs. Other engines (ChatGPT search, Perplexity) are observed to quote self-contained passages; that's industry observation, not documented behaviour. So use these only where they also help a person, on a few key passages:
- **Self-contained answers.** Right after a question, the answer in 1–2 sentences that make sense alone: name the subject instead of "it".
- **Exact facts:** numbers, dates, model names, prices, units.
- **Named comparisons** ("X vs Y", "how it differs from the previous model"), in a table when several items share attributes.
- **Questions as subheadings** on long pages only.
- Outside the text, one line for the user: AI search can only cite what its bots can crawl (OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Googlebot, YandexBot); check robots.txt doesn't block them.

## Check
Do the title, text, structured data and feed state the same facts?
