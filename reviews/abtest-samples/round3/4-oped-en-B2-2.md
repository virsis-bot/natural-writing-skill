# Pay for Wi-Fi when the cook takes hours. Otherwise, keep your money.

*By Dana Whitfield, head of product at Halden Kitchen*

I sell kitchenware for a living, and most of the time my advice on smart appliances is to buy the cheaper one.

Take the air fryer. At Halden, the app-connected version typically costs $40–60 more than the same model without Wi-Fi. Same model, so the whole difference is the connection, and the fair question is what you get for it. After nine years as head of product here, I'd rather answer that with numbers than with a feature list.

In 2025 we surveyed 1,200 people after they had bought connected appliances. 61% had connected the app at least once. Turn that around: roughly four in ten paid for a feature and never switched it on.

It gets worse with time. Three months after purchase, 18% were still using the app. That's about 216 people out of 1,200.

And what were they doing with it? The most common use was a notification that cooking is done. I don't want to sneer at that. A ping on your phone is pleasant, and if you're in the far end of the house, maybe it saves you a cold dinner. But it is a modest thing to pay $40–60 for, and it's the top of the list, the use people reach for more than any other.

Then there are the returns, which are the part I'd most like you to think about before you check out. Connected models came back 1.4 times as often as their non-connected versions, and the top reason was "the app wouldn't pair." Look at what that complaint is about. It says nothing about how the thing cooks. Someone paid extra for a feature, couldn't get the feature to start, and ended up with a return to deal with instead of dinner. The upgrade became the reason the appliance left the house.

If that were the entire story, I'd tell you to skip smart appliances altogether. It isn't, because one category in our survey behaved completely differently.

Among buyers of connected sous-vide cookers, 54% were still using the app after three months. That's three times the overall figure, and since those sous-vide buyers are counted in the overall figure, everything else must sit below 18%.

I don't think sous-vide owners are a more patient or more technical species of cook. I think it comes down to the clock. A sous-vide cook takes hours, and you aren't going to stand beside it for all of them. Being able to check on it and adjust it from your phone solves a problem you really have, on the day you have it. The app stays on the phone because it keeps being useful, which is about the only reason any app does.

So here's the test I'd use, and it has little to do with the appliance's spec sheet: will this thing be cooking for so long that I'll want to deal with it from somewhere else? If yes, the connection is earning its price. If the app would mostly tell you something you were about to find out anyway, buy the plain version and spend the difference on something you'll touch every week.

I should say what our survey can't tell you. It measured whether people kept using the app, and that isn't the same as whether they were glad they bought it. Somewhere in that 18% are people who love the ping and would pay for it again, and if you already know you're one of them, go ahead. It's your kitchen, and it's a small sum for something that makes you happy.

We still sell both versions of most models, so you don't have to take my word for any of this or give up the option either way. If you do go connected, here's my one practical request: keep the box until the app has paired.
