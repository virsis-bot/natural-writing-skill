# Skip the Wi-Fi on your air fryer

*By Dana Whitfield, head of product at Halden Kitchen*

Are smart kitchen appliances worth paying extra for? Mostly, no. That's an awkward thing to write after nine years as head of product at a company that sells them, but we asked our own customers, and I can't read their answers any other way.

In 2025 we surveyed 1,200 people after they'd bought a connected appliance from us. 61% had connected the app at least once, so roughly four in ten bought the feature and never set it up. Three months later, 18% were still using it. Out of 1,200 people, that's about 216.

And what were people doing in the app? The most common use was getting a notification that cooking was done.

Put that next to the price. Here at Halden, the Wi-Fi version of an air fryer typically costs $40 to $60 more than the same model without it. If all you want from the app is a ping when dinner's ready, you've bought a $40 timer, and to use it you need a phone, which already has a timer on it.

Being ignored is the mild outcome. The one that bothers me more shows up in our returns: connected models came back 1.4 times as often as their non-connected versions, and the top reason was "the app wouldn't pair." Think about what that means for the person involved. They paid extra for a feature, the feature failed at the very first step, and the whole appliance went back in the box. Someone wanted an air fryer and got an errand. The cheaper model has no pairing step to fail.

If the story ended there, this would be a short post telling you to never buy anything with Wi-Fi in it. But one number in the survey didn't fit. Among buyers of connected sous-vide cookers, 54% were still using the app after three months, three times the overall figure.

I don't think sous-vide people are more patient with apps than the rest of us. I think the app does something for them. A sous-vide cook takes hours, and during those hours you'd probably rather not stand over a pot of water, so being able to check on it and adjust it from your phone is useful. Nobody needs that for a basket of fries.

That gives me the one question I'd ask before paying for any connected appliance: is this job long enough that I'll want to check on it, or change something, while I'm away from it? If yes, the app has real work to do, and the sous-vide numbers suggest you'll keep using it. If the best use you can come up with is being told that the food is ready, keep the money.

There's a fair objection to all this, which is that survey percentages describe a crowd and you're one person. Those 216 people exist, and some of them may love their connected appliances. If you've owned one before and kept using the app long after the novelty wore off, you know who you are, and you should buy what you like. It's your kitchen. My only caution is that I'd guess plenty of the people who drifted away also expected to be in the 18% on the day they bought.

We still sell both versions of most models, so nobody here is taking the choice away from you, and the Wi-Fi one will be right there on the site if you decide you want it. But when you've got two listings open for the same air fryer, the one without Wi-Fi is the one I'd put in my cart.
