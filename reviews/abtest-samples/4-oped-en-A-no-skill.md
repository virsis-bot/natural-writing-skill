# Are Smart Kitchen Appliances Worth Paying Extra For? Mostly, No

*By Dana Whitfield, Head of Product, Halden Kitchen*

I've been head of product at Halden Kitchen for nine years, and choosing what we stock is my job. So it might seem odd for me to tell you not to buy some of it. But I think most of you shouldn't pay extra for the Wi-Fi version of a kitchen appliance. There's one clear exception, and I'll come to it.

Start with the price. On our site, the app-connected version of an air fryer usually costs $40 to $60 more than the same model without Wi-Fi. It's the same model. The extra money pays for a chip, an app and the promise that your kitchen will be a bit smarter.

We wanted to know whether that promise holds up, so in 2025 we surveyed 1,200 people who had bought connected appliances from us. We asked them after the purchase, once the box was open and the thing was on the counter.

Here's what they told us. 61% had connected the app at least once. Put the other way, almost four in ten paid for a feature they never even set up. Three months later, 18% were still using the app. That leaves more than four out of five buyers carrying a price premium for something that, by then, did nothing for them.

What did the people who stuck with it actually use the app for? Mostly for a notification that the food was done. That is a real convenience, but a modest one. An air fryer finishes in minutes, and a timer on the phone you already own does the same job for free.

Then there are returns. Connected models came back to us 1.4 times as often as their non-connected versions, and the most common reason was "the app wouldn't pair." So some people paid more for a feature they couldn't get working, and then had to pack the appliance up and send it back. The Wi-Fi didn't make that air fryer better. It added one more thing that could go wrong.

None of this means the connected models are bad products. The trouble is that most people don't use the part they paid extra for, and a fair number of them run into trouble with it.

## The exception: sous-vide

Sous-vide cookers are different, and the numbers show it. Among buyers of connected sous-vide models, 54% were still using the app after three months. That's three times the rate across connected appliances overall.

The reason is how sous-vide works. A cook can take hours. You put the food in the water bath and leave it, sometimes for most of the afternoon. Being able to check the temperature and adjust it from your phone, without going back to the kitchen, is genuinely useful over that kind of stretch. The app solves a problem that sous-vide cooking actually has. A twenty-minute air fryer cycle doesn't have that problem.

I think that's the test worth applying to any smart appliance. Does the app do something you'd otherwise have to get up and walk to the kitchen for, over a long enough time that it matters? For sous-vide, yes. For most of the countertop gear we sell, honestly, no.

## So why do we sell both?

Halden still offers both versions of most models, and I don't plan to change that. Almost one buyer in five was still using the app three months in, and those people should be able to buy it. For a sous-vide cooker, I'd point most people toward the connected one.

For everything else, my advice is simple. Think about how you cook now. If you can't name something specific you'd do with the app, beyond getting a "done" alert, buy the standard model and keep the $40 to $60. Our own numbers suggest you won't miss it.
