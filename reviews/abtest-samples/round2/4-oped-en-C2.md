# Most smart kitchen appliances aren't worth the extra money. One is

*By Dana Whitfield, head of product at Halden Kitchen*

On our site, the Wi-Fi version of an air fryer usually costs $40 to $60 more than the same model without it. What that money buys is an app. I've spent nine years deciding what Halden sells, and my advice for most appliances is to keep the $40.

This isn't a hunch. In 2025 we surveyed 1,200 people who had bought a connected appliance from us. 61% had connected the app at least once, which means almost four in ten paid for a feature they never switched on. Three months after buying, 18% were still using the app. Put the other way round: out of every hundred people who paid extra, about 82 had either stopped using what they paid for or never started.

And what were the people who stayed with it doing? Mostly getting a notification that their food was done. That's handy, and I don't want to sneer at it. But a timer on your phone does the same job without a pairing step, and it doesn't cost $40.

The connected versions also cause more trouble. They came back to us 1.4 times as often as the same models without Wi-Fi, and the most common reason on the return form was "the app wouldn't pair." So some customers paid more for a feature, couldn't get it to work, and sent the whole appliance back. That's a wasted afternoon for them and a cost for us, and it's part of why I'm writing this instead of quietly selling the pricier version.

There is one place where I'd tell you to pay: sous-vide. Among buyers of our connected sous-vide cookers, 54% were still using the app after three months, three times the rate across all connected appliances. The reason is the cooking itself. A sous-vide cook runs for hours, and nobody wants to stand next to a pot of water all afternoon. Being able to check on it from your phone while you're out, and adjust it if you need to, is exactly the kind of thing an app is good at. Telling you your fries are ready while you're in the same room is not.

That gives you a simple test for anything with a Wi-Fi badge on the listing. Ask two questions: how long does this appliance run while I'm not watching it, and would I ever change something halfway through if my phone told me what was happening? If it finishes before you'd have wandered off, and the app's main trick is announcing that it's finished, you're paying for a notification. If it runs for hours and you might want to step in, the app can pay for itself in the meals you don't have to babysit.

You might reasonably ask why a retailer would talk people out of the more expensive option. Partly it's the returns: a sale that comes back in a box isn't much of a sale. Partly it's that the 18% are real, and some of them clearly get value from the app. That's why Halden still sells both versions of most models. I'd rather you had the choice and made it with our numbers in front of you.

So here's how I'd choose. If you're looking at two listings for the same air fryer and the only differences are the Wi-Fi line and the price, buy the cheaper one. If it's a sous-vide cooker, get the connected one and use the app.
