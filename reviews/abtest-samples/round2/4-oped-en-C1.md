# Most kitchen appliances don't need Wi-Fi

*By Dana Whitfield, head of product at Halden Kitchen*

We sell most of our appliances in two versions, and often the only difference between them is a Wi-Fi chip and an app. On an air fryer, that difference typically costs $40 to $60 at Halden. I've run product here for nine years, and after looking at what our customers actually do with those apps, my advice for most appliances is to buy the cheaper one.

In 2025 we surveyed 1,200 people after they had bought a connected appliance from us. 61% had connected the app at least once. Three months after purchase, 18% were still using it. Put the other way round: of the people who went to the trouble of setting the app up, fewer than a third were still opening it three months later. Everyone else paid extra for something they tried for a while and dropped, or never switched on at all.

So what were the remaining users doing with it? Mostly getting a notification that the food was done. That's a reasonable thing to want, but the machine already beeps, and an air fryer runs for about as long as it takes to set the table. If you're in the kitchen or the next room, you'll hear it. Paying $50 to have the beep arrive on your phone instead is a choice you're free to make. I wouldn't make it.

Our returns point the same way. Connected models came back to us 1.4 times as often as the same models without Wi-Fi, and the most common reason customers gave was "the app wouldn't pair." That means the extra money buys a feature most people stop using, plus a better chance that the whole appliance goes back in the box. If you paid for the app and the app won't connect, you didn't get what you paid for, and sending it back is a fair response. I'd rather people didn't end up in that spot in the first place.

There is one clear exception in our data, and it's sous-vide. Among buyers of connected sous-vide cookers, 54% were still using the app after three months, three times the rate across all connected appliances. The reason isn't hard to see. A sous-vide cook takes hours, and over those hours you may want to check the water temperature or change the settings without standing over the pot. The app lets you do that from your phone. For an air fryer, the app mostly repeats what the machine already tells you. For a sous-vide cooker, it lets you get on with your afternoon.

That gives a simple test you can apply to anything in our catalog that comes in a connected version. How long does it run without you, and would you want to check on it or adjust it while you're somewhere else? If the answer is minutes, and you'll be in earshot, the app is unlikely to earn its price. If it's hours, the app starts doing real work, and the extra cost is easier to justify.

You might fairly ask why, if I think this, Halden still sells both versions of most models. Some people do get value from the app, and the 18% who stuck with it aren't wrong; they found it useful, and they should be able to buy it. But the numbers are also the reason we haven't dropped the cheaper versions. For most of our customers, the version without Wi-Fi is the better buy.

So if you're choosing between two versions of the same air fryer, get the one without Wi-Fi and keep the $40 to $60. If you're buying a sous-vide cooker, the connected model is the one I'd pay extra for.
