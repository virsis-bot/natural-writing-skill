# Most smart kitchen appliances aren't worth the extra money. One kind is.

*By Dana Whitfield, head of product at Halden Kitchen*

If you're looking at two air fryers on our site and one costs $40 to $60 more because it connects to Wi-Fi, buy the cheaper one. I've been head of product here for nine years, we sell both, and our own numbers make the case against the app better than I could.

In 2025 we surveyed 1,200 people who had bought a connected appliance from us. We asked a simple thing: did you actually use the app?

61% had connected it at least once. That means close to four in ten paid for Wi-Fi and never switched it on. Of the people who did set it up, most drifted away. Three months after purchase, 18% were still using the app. Out of 1,200 buyers, that's roughly 216 people.

And what were those people using it for? Mostly to get a notification on their phone that the food was done.

I don't want to sneer at that. A ping when dinner's ready is nice. But an air fryer already beeps, and you're usually standing a few feet from it, because an air fryer cook takes minutes, not hours. Paying an extra $50 for a second beep that arrives on your phone is a hard sell, and most of our customers seem to have reached the same conclusion on their own, about a month or two after checkout.

Then there's the returns data, which surprised me more than the survey did. Connected models came back 1.4 times as often as the same models without Wi-Fi. The most common reason people gave wasn't that the food was bad or the basket was too small. It was "the app wouldn't pair." So the feature that justified the higher price was also the thing most likely to send the box back to us. For a lot of buyers, the smart part didn't make the appliance better. It made the first evening with it worse.

Why do we still sell connected versions, then? Partly because some people really do want them, and it's not my job to decide that for you. And partly because of sous-vide.

Sous-vide cookers are the exception in our data, and a clear one. 54% of people who bought a connected sous-vide were still using the app three months later. Compare that with 18% across connected appliances in general.

The reason isn't hard to see once you think about how sous-vide works. A cook can run for hours. You put a steak or a pork shoulder in the water bath and then you go do something else, maybe leave the house. Being able to check the temperature from your phone, or adjust it, or know exactly when it's finished without walking back to the kitchen, solves a real problem. With an air fryer the app answers a question you weren't asking. With a sous-vide it answers the one you'd be asking all afternoon.

That, to me, is the whole test. Before you pay extra for the connected version of anything, ask how long you'll be away from it while it cooks. If the answer is "I won't be," the app is a remote control for a machine you can already reach. If the answer is "most of the day," the app might be the most useful thing about it.

So here's where I land. For short-cook appliances like air fryers, skip Wi-Fi and keep the $40 to $60. You'll get the same cooking, one less thing to pair, and, if our returns are any guide, a lower chance of packing it back into its box. For a sous-vide cooker, the connected version is worth a serious look, and it's the one I'd buy.

We'll keep selling both versions of most models, because people cook differently and some of you will use the app every day. I'd just rather you pay for it knowing that most buyers don't.
